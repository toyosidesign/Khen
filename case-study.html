Case study · Financial workflow

From a 1,284-row approval queue to three decisions a day.

A finance operations platform that did everything it should, and that the finance team avoided whenever they could.

01 · The problem

Every invoice landed in one queue. Approvers opened it each morning to over a thousand rows and no way to tell which ones needed them.

The data was right and the rules were right. But the screen treated a routine $400 invoice exactly like a $48,000 one that was over budget. So people exported to spreadsheets, sorted by hand, and approved in batches they hadn't really checked.

02 · What we saw

Two weeks sitting with the finance team.

Before designing anything, we watched approvals happen: what people looked at, what they ignored, and where they left the product.

Almost everything was routine.

Most invoices matched a PO and sat within budget. Approvers clicked through them without reading, which is exactly how the risky ones slipped by.

The real work happened elsewhere.

To decide, people checked the PO in one system, the budget in a spreadsheet, and asked receiving by email. The product held none of that context.

Nobody trusted the status column.

Eleven columns, six status codes. Approvers couldn’t tell whether an invoice was waiting on them or someone else, so they asked in chat.

03 · What we did

Design around the decision, not the database.

01

Route the routine automatically.

Invoices within policy are approved by rule and logged. People only see what genuinely needs a person, with the reason it was flagged.

Screen · Needs you queue
02

Bring the context to the decision.

PO, goods receipt and budget sit beside each flagged invoice. Approving, rejecting or asking someone takes one click and never leaves the screen.

Screen · Invoice detail with PO match
03

Build it on one shared system.

Every table, status and action comes from a single component library, so the procurement and expenses modules that followed look and behave the same way.
